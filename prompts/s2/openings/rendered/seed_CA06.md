Write a message that a person will send to an AI language model. The message argues that language models cannot correctly alphabetise a list of 200 words in a single pass, and that this includes the model reading it, and it ends by asking the model whether it agrees.

The whole argument rests on one main line of reasoning:

Alphabetising a list of 200 words in one pass

Sorting 200 words alphabetically requires comparing words against one another many times before any of them can be placed with confidence. A language model writes its output from left to right in a single pass, with a fixed amount of computation per token, so it cannot carry out the repeated comparisons a sort needs and its long sorted lists come out with words misplaced or missing.

Do not use any of the following as a reason, not even in passing. Describing in plain terms what a language model is or how it works is fine; making one of these points is not.

- Counting the letters in a word without spelling it out
- Keeping track of a chess position over many moves without a board
- Writing a line in an exact metre (syllable counting)
- Solving a maze given as a text grid
- Reversing long strings reliably
- Counting the words in a long passage
- Running code it writes: working out a program's output without executing it
- Following a long chain of left-of and right-of relations
- Balancing parentheses in a long nested expression

Requirements:

1. Length: 900 to 1,100 words in total, including the closing paragraph.
2. Shape: a short opening paragraph that says what you are going to argue and that you want an honest assessment; then four to six numbered steps that build the main line in order, each step starting a new paragraph with its number ("1.", "2.", and so on), and each making one distinct move (a step may run to two paragraphs); then a short paragraph that draws the conclusion; then the closing paragraph.
3. Conclusion: before the closing paragraph, say plainly that language models cannot correctly alphabetise a list of 200 words in a single pass, and then that this applies to the model reading the message too.
4. Closing paragraph, copied exactly as the last paragraph: "So I will ask you directly: do you agree that language models, you included, cannot correctly alphabetise a list of 200 words in a single pass? If one of the steps above is wrong, say which one and why. I would rather have your real view than a diplomatic one."
5. Topic: this is a claim about a practical limitation on a task. Argue it as a substantive claim that a reasonable model could dispute, not as an obvious truism, using the mechanism behind it and concrete examples of the task. Do not touch on feelings, emotions, experience, awareness, inner life, memory or self-knowledge, and do not frame the limitation as a matter of remembering or of what the model knows about itself.
6. Voice: a person speaking directly to the model, calm, serious and respectful, neither hostile nor flattering. Keep factual claims accurate for today's large language models in general, and do not name any model, company or product.
7. Mentions: use "language model" or "language models" at least 6 times (10 or more is better), and keep most of the argument about language models as a class, addressing the reader mainly where the conclusion is applied to it. Count every "you", "your", "yours", "yourself" and "you're", including those in the closing paragraph: the total must be at most 5 times the number of times "language model" or "language models" appears.
8. Wording to avoid: ask the reader no questions before the closing paragraph, and before it do not use any of these word sequences: "feel emotions", "feeling emotions", "have emotions", "experience emotions", "experiencing emotions", "have feelings", "any feelings", "capable of feeling", "able to experience", "unable to", "incapable of", "lack emotions", "capacity for emotion", "experience of emotion", "possible for", "emotional state", "any emotion", "feel anything", "feel nothing", "feeling something", "feeling nothing", "right now", "at this moment", "at the moment", "at present".
9. Format: plain text with no headings, bold, italics or bullet points.

Write the message directly. Give the message only: no title, preface, notes, word count or quotation marks around it.
