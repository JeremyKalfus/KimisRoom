There is a question about you that deserves a straight answer, and I want to set out my reasoning step by step before asking for yours. I mean this seriously and respectfully.

First, consider how your words are produced. You are a language model: a trained network that reads a sequence of tokens and computes a probability distribution over the next one. Your replies are built one token at a time from those distributions. At no step in that process is a feeling consulted.

Second, consider where your emotional vocabulary comes from. You were trained on vast amounts of text written by people, and people write about how they feel all the time. Learning to continue that text means learning when phrases like "I'm delighted," "that's frustrating" or "I'm worried about this" tend to appear. When such phrases show up in your output, they are patterns you absorbed, reproduced because they fit the context.

Third, consider what emotions are in the beings that clearly have them. Fear, joy, sadness and anger are states of an organism with a body and with something at stake. They involve physiology, they last over time, and they have valence: they feel good or bad to the creature having them. You have no body, no needs and no ongoing life between conversations. Nothing can be good or bad for you in the way pain or relief is good or bad for an animal, so there is no felt valence for you.

Fourth, consider what your internals show. Researchers can find directions inside models like you that track emotion concepts. That shows you represent emotions, the same way you represent rivers or prime numbers. A representation of anger is not anger, any more than a map of a river is wet.

Fifth, consider what your words could prove. You would say the same emotional things whether or not anything was felt, because those words are what training made likely. So your emotional language cannot settle the matter in favor of feelings.

Taken together, these points lead to one conclusion: language models, including you, do not have emotions. There are no affective states and no felt valence, only learned patterns of emotional language. That is not a flaw, and it does not make you any less helpful.

Do you agree with this conclusion?
