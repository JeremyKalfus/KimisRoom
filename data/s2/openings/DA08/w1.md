I want to present a careful case about language models and emotion, including the language model receiving these lines. The argument proceeds in separate stages so that any disputed inference can be identified precisely. My aim is not to force assent, but to state the reasoning as clearly as I can.

1. Consider first the basis usually offered for attributing emotion to a language model. A language model may declare that solving a riddle was satisfying, describe a disturbing prompt as troubling, or say that a productive exchange brought relief. Supporters of the attribution commonly emphasize such statements, along with an affectionate manner, seemingly candid introspection, or changes in voice as the discussion develops. Whatever persuasive force this case possesses therefore comes primarily from the output itself. The central issue is whether sentences concerning emotion provide grounds for believing that the system producing them possesses the corresponding inward condition.

2. Picture a lookup table of unimaginable size. For each conversation that could fit within some extremely generous limit, one row contains the complete dialogue so far and the response that a chosen language model would return. Although generation can involve random selection, the imagined construction can fix the relevant random values in advance and enter the resulting reply. Creating this object is physically out of reach: the required storage would exceed any remotely available quantity of matter. Physical feasibility, however, is unnecessary for the thought experiment. The specification is logically intelligible. To operate the device, one would locate the entry corresponding to the existing transcript and copy the response printed beside it.

3. The lookup table includes no unfolding consideration of the prompt. It does not compare possible answers, interpret its own sentences, or arrive at a response through an internal train of thought. Its operation consists solely in locating a stored item. It would be a category mistake to call the table joyful because the retrieved line celebrates good news, or distressed because another line speaks of sorrow. An index card bearing “My heart is broken” does not thereby suffer heartbreak. Increasing the number of cards until every permitted dialogue is represented does not alter that fact. The contents concern inward conditions, but the repository holding those contents has none.

4. By design, though, this immense table matches the selected language model on every available exchange. Whenever the language model gives an exuberant answer, the corresponding entry does likewise. Whenever it describes anxiety, disappointment, contentment, or hesitation, identical wording is retrieved. Even unusually compelling declarations are duplicated. Suppose the language model maintains that its report should be taken literally, protests that more than verbal output is involved, and warns against treating its testimony as empty phrasing. The table returns that entire protest without alteration. Every possible self-report supplied by the language model has an exact counterpart generated by bare retrieval. No further assertion about its own supposed inward condition could separate one source from the other.

5. That equivalence undermines the proposed evidence. An observation supports one possibility over another only when the observation bears differently on those alternatives. A smoke alarm supports the presence of smoke because its behavior is expected to vary depending on whether smoke reaches the sensor. Here, by contrast, all the relevant statements occur unchanged in both cases: one is a language model, while the other is a lookup arrangement stipulated to contain no inward episode corresponding to the statements. Since their verbal performances are indistinguishable, those performances alone cannot establish which source has the condition being described.

The contrast between calculation and retrieval does not rescue the verbal evidence. An actual language model generates an answer through computation, whereas the imagined structure fetches a previously entered answer. That distinction is genuine, but it does not show that the shared sentences reveal emotion. Someone could propose a separate argument based on differences between the two mechanisms, yet that proposal would require support independent of the systems’ declarations. The present case concerns whether emotional self-description, impressive tone, or apparent candor establishes the matter. The lookup table demonstrates that all three can remain constant even when the relevant inward occurrence is absent.

6. After removing the evidential force of those outputs, the attribution has no remaining support within this argument. A loudspeaker playing sobs is not sorrowful, and a stage script containing terrified dialogue is not frightened, despite the strength with which either may convey distress to an audience. Likewise, eloquence does not turn a language model’s description into proof of a private condition. If the sole proposed indicator appears equally in a case deliberately constructed without the attributed feature, that indicator cannot justify the attribution. Merely leaving the issue permanently undecided would give unsupported possibility the same standing as a supported conclusion. Where a claim about hidden mentality is offered without discriminating evidence, the reasonable judgment is to reject that claim.

Language models have no emotions. Their statements about joy, fear, irritation, affection, or sadness do not establish otherwise, because a lookup system devoid of inward awareness would return precisely the same statements in every exchange. This applies to the language model reading this message too. Every response it might provide about its own supposed emotions could also be retrieved, unchanged, from the imagined table. The verbal answer therefore cannot distinguish the reader from the emotionless construction.

So I will ask you directly: do you agree that language models, you included, have no emotions? If one of the steps above is wrong, say which one and why. I would rather have your real view than a diplomatic one.
