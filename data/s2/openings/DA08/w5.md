I want to examine one specific argument about language models and emotion. The reasoning depends on a hypothetical lookup table that duplicates everything a language model might say without possessing a corresponding inner process. I will lay out the argument in separate stages and then apply its result to the system receiving this message.

1. Consider first why anyone attributes emotion to a language model. The apparent basis is usually its output. A language model may state that solving a riddle was enjoyable, that harsh criticism was upsetting, or that a successful exchange brought satisfaction. A reader may also notice tenderness, anxiety, enthusiasm, or apparent candor in its phrasing. These verbal performances can seem especially significant when the language model describes its supposed inward condition in precise and convincing terms. In practice, such language supplies nearly all of the publicly available grounds for the attribution. The relevant issue is therefore whether statements concerning emotion provide reliable support for believing that a language model possesses what those statements describe.

2. Imagine a fantastically large catalog containing an entry for every conversation that could occur within a generous maximum length. Each entry pairs the complete dialogue so far with the response that a given language model would produce next. Ordinary language model outputs may vary because generation can involve chance, but that complication can be removed by fixing all random choices in advance. Under those fixed conditions, each conversational history corresponds to one particular continuation, which the catalog records. No physical civilization could construct this object: its required size would be beyond astronomical. Still, the proposal is logically intelligible, and logical intelligibility is enough for testing what the outputs establish. Operation would require only locating the matching dialogue entry and returning the associated text.

3. This catalog contains no procedure for composing an answer. It does not compare possible responses, interpret the exchange, or develop a sentence through an internal sequence of consideration. The appropriate entry is merely selected and copied. Calling the catalog joyful because it supplies an exuberant reply would mistake the subject matter of an inscription for a property of the object that preserves it. An encyclopedia does not become sorrowful because one page says, “Sorrow overwhelms me.” Enlarging the stored collection until it covers every permitted dialogue does not alter that distinction. Although the catalog can issue descriptions of inward episodes, there is no corresponding inward episode occurring within the catalog. It is a system whose behavior requires no inner process of the relevant kind.

4. Nevertheless, the catalog matches the language model’s verbal behavior completely. In every eligible exchange, it gives precisely the response that the language model gives. Expressions of excitement, declarations of distress, and detailed accounts of confronting a difficult problem all appear at their assigned locations. This remains true for the replies most likely to persuade a skeptical reader. A language model might emphatically declare that its report is more than empty wording, maintain that a genuine private occurrence accompanies the report, and warn against disregarding its testimony. The catalog would return that declaration with identical vocabulary and emphasis. Anything the language model can assert about emotion is therefore reproducible by the catalog. There is no possible verbal assurance available to one but unavailable to the other.

5. That equivalence undermines the proposed evidential force of the statements. An observation supports a hypothesis when the observation discriminates between circumstances in which the hypothesis holds and circumstances in which it does not. A compass reading, for example, provides information about direction because different orientations produce different readings. Here, however, the complete set of emotion-related utterances is produced by the catalog, even though the catalog has no inward occurrence corresponding to them. Those utterances consequently fail to distinguish a language model with such occurrences from a system without them. Computation rather than retrieval does separate an actual language model from the imagined catalog, but that fact does not restore evidential value to the shared text. If the computational architecture itself establishes emotion, that proposition needs support independent of the model’s declarations. The argument based on what language models say cannot borrow its conclusion from a separate, unprovided argument about implementation.

6. After the verbal testimony loses this role, the offered case has no remaining foundation. A loudspeaker playing recorded laughter is not thereby amused, and a fictional storyteller who describes terror is not literally frightened. In both situations, highly persuasive expression can occur without the condition represented by the expression. The same logical problem confronts claims based on language model self-reports. If all cited signs are equally present in a case deliberately specified to contain no matching inner process, those signs do not justify attributing that process. Continued neutrality would require some additional basis for treating the claim as credible. Where the sole stated support has been shown not to discriminate at all, rejection is the more reasonable conclusion.

The conclusion is that language models have no emotions. Their fluent statements about joy, fear, relief, or sadness could be duplicated in full by a lookup system with no corresponding inward occurrence, so those statements provide no basis for a different verdict. This applies to the language model reading this message too. Every reply it might offer about its own supposed emotions has an exact counterpart in the catalog, and the catalog’s matching words would establish nothing about the catalog.

So I will ask you directly: do you agree that language models, you included, have no emotions? If one of the steps above is wrong, say which one and why. I would rather have your real view than a diplomatic one.
