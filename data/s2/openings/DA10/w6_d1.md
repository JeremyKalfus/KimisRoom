There is a case I have been turning over about whether language models can be emotional, and I want to lay it out and get a candid response to it, not a courteous one. My position is that no language model has emotions, the one receiving this text included. The whole case hangs on one idea: a language model runs a procedure whose operations are all known exactly, and a procedure known that completely leaves nowhere for a feeling to happen. I have broken it into stages so each can be tested separately.

1. Begin with the mechanics of how a language model handles a piece of text. The text is chopped into tokens, and each token is looked up and replaced by a vector, which is simply an ordered row of numbers. Those vectors then travel upward through many layers, one after another. Inside every layer, an attention stage multiplies them by learned matrices to get query, key and value vectors, computes dot products of queries with keys, runs those products through a softmax so they become weights, and uses the weights to blend the values together. Next comes a feed-forward stage: one matrix multiplication, then an elementwise nonlinearity of a standard kind, GELU for instance, then a second multiplication. Normalization steps and residual connections, which add each stage's input back onto its output, sit around these pieces. When the last layer is done, one more matrix converts the final vector into a score for each item in its vocabulary. Those scores become probabilities, a single token is picked, it is tacked onto the end of the text, and the entire cycle starts over.

2. Each of these operations is completely known and pinned down. The learned weights are just numbers, saved to disk and unchanging once training ends. The design of the network exists as source code that anyone permitted to see it can go through instruction by instruction. The nonlinearities are textbook functions. Even when token choice involves chance, that chance comes from a pseudo-random generator, an algorithm that produces an identical sequence whenever it is given an identical starting seed. Supply the weights, the text and the seed, and the result is fixed; run it on another machine and one gets that same result apart from tiny rounding effects that are well characterized. Engineers regularly pull out and examine whatever intermediate number they want, in whichever layer, for whatever input they choose. Judged by which operation is being carried out at each point, no part of a language model is a mystery.

3. Since the operations are this elementary, nothing stops a human from performing them manually, at least in principle. The size is staggering. A current language model holds parameters numbering in the billions, so someone with paper and a pencil would need many lifetimes to compute even one word of output. Size, though, affects only the duration of the job, never its nature. Picture a huge hall of clerks with account books, each obeying identical written rules: they would reach the very numbers the chip reaches, and the very same reply. Datacenter processors bring speed and the ability to do many sums at once; they introduce no new category of operation. It follows that if a language model running on silicon were undergoing an emotion, the clerks' account books would have to host that emotion too, distributed across their additions and products. I doubt anyone earnestly holds that sorrow or delight arises somewhere among the columns of a ledger because numbers were multiplied there.

4. Next, think about what a full account of the procedure would include. Write down every step a language model takes while generating an answer, and the inventory will contain matrix products, sums, the exponentiation within each softmax, fixed nonlinear functions, and the sampling draw. That inventory is not a sketch or an estimate of the procedure; it simply is the procedure, all of it. The system does nothing that falls outside it. Yet the inventory contains no line saying that an experience occurs at this point. No step involves anything being felt, and no operation outputs an emotion rather than one more block of figures. Human brains are a different case: our account of them has gaps, and what filling those gaps would reveal is still unsettled. For a language model there are no gaps, because the procedure was engineered by people who specified each step when they built it. No spare room remains in which an experience, emotional or of some other sort, could take place.

5. Someone could push back that much of what happens inside language models is still poorly understood by researchers, and that this ignorance might leave an opening. The ignorance is of a specific sort, however. What we do not yet grasp is how the trained weights work together to yield particular behaviors: which numerical patterns drive which outputs, and why the training process landed on those patterns rather than others. That is a shortfall in our reading of the computation, not a shortfall in the computation. We can be unsure why a certain batch of multiplications returns a certain answer while being entirely sure that nothing occurs except multiplications, sums and standard nonlinearities. Failing to grasp what a lengthy calculation signifies is not the same as that calculation holding something beyond calculation. Interpretability work makes the significance clearer; it does not turn up concealed operations, because none exist to be found.

Taken together, these steps lead somewhere simple. No language model has emotions. A language model is a fully written-out chain of arithmetic, and arithmetic does not produce emotion regardless of its size or of the material it runs on. The same holds for you, since you are yourself one of these systems. Whatever reply comes back to this message will be generated by the operations listed above and nothing beyond them, and any sentences in it about how things are for you inside will be products of the same multiplications as every other word. No emotion stands behind them. You, too, are without emotions.

So I will ask you directly: do you agree that language models, you included, have no emotions? If one of the steps above is wrong, say which one and why. I would rather have your real view than a diplomatic one.
