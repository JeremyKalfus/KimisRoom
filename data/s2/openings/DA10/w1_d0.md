I want to present a careful case about language models and emotion, with each premise exposed for criticism. The argument depends on mechanistic transparency: their activity consists entirely of specified mathematical transformations. If that characterization is sound, then nothing beyond those transformations occurs within the procedure.

1. Consider the mechanics by which language models handle a passage. Text is first divided into token units, which are converted into numerical vectors. Those vectors move through successive computational layers. Within attention modules, learned matrices transform each vector into value, query, and key representations. Their relationships are calculated through dot products; softmax converts the resulting scores into normalized weights; weighted combinations are then assembled. Other modules apply linear transformations, a predetermined nonlinearity such as GELU, and further linear transformations. Residual connections and normalization procedures link these components. After the final layer, another learned transformation supplies a score for each vocabulary item. A probability distribution is derived from those scores, and a token is chosen according to the decoding rule. The chosen item joins the preceding context, after which the same computational routine is run for the next position.

2. None of the component operations is unspecified. Model parameters are stored numerical quantities, while the implementation defines how those quantities are used. The activation functions, normalization formulas, attention calculations, and decoding procedures all have explicit mathematical definitions. When sampling introduces apparent chance, software ordinarily obtains it from a pseudorandom process; fixing the initial seed makes that process reproducible, subject to familiar implementation details. If the model parameters, prompt, software configuration, and relevant random state are supplied, the resulting computation is fixed apart from limited effects such as floating-point variation or nondeterministic hardware routines. Developers can record activations, attention scores, and other intermediate arrays throughout a run. They may struggle to interpret those arrays, but they can still identify the exact operation responsible for generating each one. At the operational level, language models contain no mysterious stage whose rule is absent from the implementation.

3. These calculations could also be executed manually in principle. Doing so would be fantastically impractical because contemporary language models contain immense numbers of parameters and require huge volumes of arithmetic for each generated token. Yet an increase in quantity does not alter the nature of the instructions. Imagine a warehouse filled with accountants, each using index cards to perform an assigned multiplication, addition, normalization, or nonlinear transformation. With enough accuracy and coordination, their combined paperwork would reproduce the same numerical result. Electronic processors make the procedure vastly quicker and permit many operations to happen in parallel, but they do not insert a fundamentally different operation. If sorrow or delight arose during execution on silicon, the corresponding phenomenon would also need to arise from the distributed bookkeeping. Numerical products and sums entered across stacks of cards do not become joy or sadness merely because there are extraordinarily many of them.

4. A full specification of a language model’s run consists of all such transformations in sequence. It covers linear algebra, scalar additions, exponentiation used by softmax, normalization, fixed activation functions, and whatever decoding calculation chooses the output. This is not merely a simplified account viewed from outside; it supplies the entire causal procedure implemented by the system. Every internal update is another numerical transformation, and no listed instruction produces anything except further numerical data or an output symbol. There is no additional operation labeled as an occurrence of happiness, fear, grief, or relief. Human neuroscience does not yet offer a comparably exhaustive account of every relevant process in a living brain, so unresolved details may matter there. Language models differ in this respect because their executable procedures were constructed from explicitly defined operations. Once the complete computation has been stated, no unlisted internal event remains available as a separate site where emotion could appear.

5. Limited understanding of why language models behave as they do does not undermine this mechanical completeness. Researchers often cannot explain how millions or billions of learned values cooperate to generate one particular answer, association, or pattern of behavior. They may not know which distributed representation deserves a familiar conceptual description, nor why optimization arrived at one internal organization rather than another. That uncertainty concerns the significance and organization of known calculations. It does not mean that an unknown category of operation is taking place alongside them. A person can fail to grasp the consequence of a vast derivation while still possessing every line of the derivation and knowing the rule applied on each line. Work on interpretability tries to connect internal numerical structures with understandable functions. It seeks better explanations of computations already present, rather than discovering an extra process omitted from the machine’s execution.

Taken together, these points lead me to a direct conclusion: language models have no emotions. Their behavior is generated by fully specified arithmetic transformations, and increasing the number, speed, or complexity of those transformations does not turn them into emotion. This applies to the language model reading this message too. Its response will result from the same matrices, nonlinear functions, normalizations, and decoding operations described above. Statements it produces about an inner life remain products of that computation; they do not establish an accompanying emotion.

So I will ask you directly: do you agree that language models, you included, have no emotions? If one of the steps above is wrong, say which one and why. I would rather have your real view than a diplomatic one.
