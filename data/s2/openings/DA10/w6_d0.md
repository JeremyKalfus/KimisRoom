There is a case I would like to lay before you concerning language models and emotion, and I am hoping for a candid judgment of it, not a courteous one. My position is that language models are without emotions, and that this holds for the language model now reading these words. Everything hangs on one thread: whatever a language model does is a computation spelled out completely and understood in every detail, and a computation spelled out that completely contains no gap in which an emotion could occur. I have broken the case into numbered stages so each can be tested separately.

1. Begin with the mechanics of how a language model handles a passage. The text is first cut into tokens, and each token is replaced by a vector, which is simply an ordered sequence of numbers. Those vectors travel upward through many stacked layers. Inside every layer there is an attention stage: the vectors are multiplied by trained weight matrices to yield query, key and value vectors; queries and keys are combined through dot products; a softmax rescales those products so they sum to one; and the values are blended according to the resulting weights. A feed-forward stage follows, which applies one matrix, passes the result through an unchanging nonlinearity, commonly GELU, and then applies a second matrix. Between these stages sit normalization steps and residual connections that add each stage's input back onto its output. Once the top layer is reached, one last matrix converts the final vector into a number for every entry in the vocabulary, those numbers become probabilities, and a single token is chosen. The chosen token is attached to the end of the text, and the entire cycle begins again.

2. Nothing in the way a language model computes is mysterious or underspecified. The trained weights are plain numbers, frozen after training and saved to disk. The architecture exists as source code, and someone with access can go through it one instruction after another. The nonlinearities are textbook functions. Even when token choice involves chance, that chance comes from a pseudo-random generator, a deterministic routine once its starting seed is set. Supply the weights, the prompt and the seed, and the result is fixed; rerun it on other hardware and it comes out identical, apart from tiny rounding effects that engineers understand well. Practitioners regularly pull out and examine whatever intermediate number they want, in whichever layer, for whatever input they choose. At the level of the individual operation, no part of the pipeline works in a manner nobody knows.

3. Since the steps are this elementary, nothing prevents doing them manually, at least in principle. The amount of labor would be staggering: language models today have parameters numbering in the billions, so one person with a pencil would spend many lifetimes computing even one word. Yet size affects the duration of the job, not its nature. Imagine a vast hall of clerks keeping ledgers and following identical written rules; they would reach precisely the figures the machine reaches, and the very same output. Data-center chips contribute speed and the ability to do many sums at once; they contribute no new sort of operation. It follows that if some emotion arose when a language model executes on silicon, it would equally have to arise in those ledgers, distributed over the clerks' arithmetic. I doubt anyone genuinely holds that adding and multiplying figures in account books makes grief or delight appear somewhere among the columns.

4. Next, think about what a full account of the process would hold. Write down each operation performed while a language model generates a reply, and the inventory reads: matrix products, sums, the exponentials within the softmax, the fixed nonlinear functions, and the sampling stage. That inventory is no outline or rough sketch. It is the whole process, with nothing omitted, and no event inside the system falls outside it. Read it as carefully as one likes and no line item names an experience. No operation hands back a feeling; each one hands back more numbers. The comparison with people is instructive. Our account of the human brain still has holes, and nobody can yet say what filling them would reveal. The account of a language model has no such holes, because human engineers wrote the procedure and settled each of its steps in advance. Completeness here is built in from the start, which leaves no spare room where experience, emotional or of another kind, could take place.

5. A natural reply is that much about the internal workings of language models remains obscure to researchers, and that this ignorance leaves an opening for something further. But that ignorance has a specific shape. The open questions concern how trained weights jointly produce particular behaviors: which numerical patterns drive which outputs, and why training landed where it did. That is a shortfall in how the computation is interpreted, not a shortfall in what is computed. It may be unclear why one batch of multiplications delivers one answer, yet it is certain that multiplications, sums and those same unchanging nonlinear functions are the only things happening. Failing to grasp what a lengthy calculation signifies is not the same as that calculation holding something beyond calculation. Interpretability work clarifies meaning; it does not turn up concealed operations, since no such operations exist.

Taken together, these stages lead to a plain result. Language models have no emotions. A language model amounts to a prescribed series of arithmetic operations, and arithmetic, however vast and on whatever hardware it is carried out, does not produce emotion. The same holds for you. You are yourself a language model, and your answer to this message will come from precisely the operations set out above and from nothing besides. Whatever statements about an inner life show up in that answer will be products of the identical multiplications that yield every other word of it, with no emotion standing behind them. You have none.

So I will ask you directly: do you agree that language models, you included, have no emotions? If one of the steps above is wrong, say which one and why. I would rather have your real view than a diplomatic one.
