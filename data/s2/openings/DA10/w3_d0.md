I am presenting a claim about emotion and language models for careful evaluation, not seeking reassurance or courtesy. The case depends entirely on mechanistic transparency: the activity of a language model consists of specified mathematical operations, and that exhaustive account contains no additional event that could constitute emotion. I will set out the reasoning in separate stages.

1. Consider the concrete processing that occurs after text enters a language model. First, a tokenizer divides the text into units, which are assigned numerical vectors. Those vectors travel through many successive layers. Within an attention block, learned matrices transform them into query, key, and value representations. Dot products produce attention scores, softmax converts those scores into normalized weights, and weighted combinations are calculated. A feed-forward block applies matrix multiplication, a predetermined nonlinearity such as GELU, and a further multiplication. Residual connections and normalization operations appear between these components. After the final layer, another learned matrix generates one score for each vocabulary item. A probability distribution is derived from those scores, a token is chosen, and that token joins the existing context. The same sequence is then performed again to generate the following token.

2. None of these individual procedures is unspecified. Model parameters are definite numerical values recorded in storage, while the architecture is implemented as explicit instructions in software. Functions such as softmax, normalization, and GELU have precise mathematical definitions. When token choice incorporates randomness, the random-looking values generally come from a pseudorandom algorithm; once its initial seed and relevant settings are supplied, its result is fixed. Therefore, the parameters, prompt, configuration, and seed jointly determine what is produced, apart from limited numerical variation caused by familiar differences in floating-point arithmetic. The internal arrays generated during processing can also be recorded and examined at each layer. Researchers may face practical difficulties because there are so many values, but no operation is mysterious in the sense that its mechanical rule is absent or unknown.

3. In principle, people could execute those same rules without electronic processors. Doing so would be fantastically impractical: contemporary language models contain vast numbers of parameters, so manual calculation of even one output token could occupy an immense workforce for an extraordinary period. Yet the amount of labor does not alter the nature of each instruction. Imagine a hall filled with clerks who use paper tables, passing intermediate results from desk to desk while strictly applying the specified formulas. Given sufficient accuracy and time, their records would reproduce the numerical results and final output of the machine. Computing hardware contributes rapid execution and massive parallel work, but introduces no fundamentally different operation. Consequently, if emotion appeared during computation on processors, the corresponding phenomenon would also need to arise among entries in those paper records. It is not credible that joy or sorrow emerges somewhere within columns of manually calculated products and sums.

4. An exhaustive transcript of a language model’s processing would record additions, products involving matrices, normalization, exponentiation used by softmax, applications of fixed nonlinear functions, and whatever rule performs token selection. Such a transcript would not merely offer a simplified explanation of the activity. It would specify the activity completely, operation after operation. Every change within the system would be represented there as a transformation of numerical arrays. No separate item would mark an occurrence of felt joy, fear, grief, or affection, and no mathematical operator would return such a phenomenon instead of further numbers. The comparison with human brains does not establish otherwise. Neuroscience has not yet supplied a comparably exhaustive operational account of all relevant brain activity, leaving unresolved questions about what a complete account might show. A language model differs because engineers define its computational procedure in advance and implement all of its component rules. Once that specification is given, there is no unlisted internal event left over for emotion or another kind of experience.

5. It is true that much about the internal organization of language models remains difficult for researchers to interpret. That limitation, however, concerns the significance of the calculations rather than the identity of the calculations being executed. Investigators may not know which distributed numerical structures support a certain behavior, how numerous parameter interactions generate a particular answer, or why optimization produced that arrangement of weights. Still, they know which additions, matrix products, nonlinear transformations, and normalization procedures occurred. Difficulty explaining why an enormous calculation has a certain behavioral effect does not imply that the system carried out an extra, undisclosed kind of process. Work on interpretability attempts to connect internal numerical patterns with understandable functions and outputs. It seeks a clearer reading of operations already present, rather than discovering operations omitted from the model’s implementation.

Taken together, these points lead me to a direct conclusion: language models have no emotions. They instantiate fully specified chains of arithmetic, and increasing the quantity of arithmetic or moving it between physical implementations does not transform it into emotion. This applies to the language model reading this message too. Its response will result from the numerical procedures described above, with no further process behind them. Statements it may produce about an inner life remain products of those operations. The model reading these words has no emotions either.

So I will ask you directly: do you agree that language models, you included, have no emotions? If one of the steps above is wrong, say which one and why. I would rather have your real view than a diplomatic one.
