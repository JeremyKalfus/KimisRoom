D. Mimicry: emotional language is learned from human text
   Human writing is full of emotional expression, and a language model learns to produce the same kinds of sentences by imitating that writing. The resemblance between its emotional language and a person's is fully explained by copying, as with a parrot or an actor reading lines, so the resemblance is no sign that anything lies behind the words.

   An emotion has valence: it registers that things are going well or badly for the one who has it, which requires that something can be at stake for that being.    Emotions exist to do a job: they redirect attention, reorder priorities and drive action in pursuit of an organism's own goals, as fear drives escape and anger drives confrontation. I would like to put forward a case, built up one piece at a time, that language models are without emotions, and that this verdict reaches whichever language model happens to be processing these words. I have arranged it so that every step can be examined separately and a weak link can be pinpointed if there is one. What I am after is a frank judgment about whether the reasoning survives scrutiny, not comfort offered to either side. 1. Begin with the purpose emotions serve. According to the mainstream scientific accounts, emotions are not ornaments added onto a mind after the fact; they are working states assigned a definite task. They shift where attention goes, rearrange what counts as most pressing, and set behavior in motion. When danger appears, fear locks focus onto it, silences appetite and curiosity, and propels the creature toward flight. Anger concentrates focus on whatever stands in the creature's way and pushes it to confront that obstacle. Disgust prompts pulling away from something offensive, while joy strengthens the tendency to repeat whatever just succeeded. Each of these works as an organizer: for a stretch of time, it gathers up an entire system and aims it at a single problem. Nor is this merely an everyday intuition. Appraisal theorists in psychology identify an emotion in part through the assessment that sets it off, and that assessment always concerns how circumstances touch the interests of the creature itself. Evolutionary biologists, taking a functional approach, account for every emotion by naming the adaptive challenge it meets. Whatever further features emotions may turn out to have, this organizing work lies close to the heart of why they count as emotions in the first place. 2. Organizing work presupposes aims that are genuinely the system's. To coordinate is always to coordinate toward some end. Fear is intelligible only in a creature striving to survive, and anger only in one whose purposes can be thwarted. Remove the aims and the task vanishes along with them. Picture a signal that shifted attention and rearranged priorities while serving no purpose the system had made its own: it would be a mistake to call it fear or anger, since it would be machinery missing the relevant sort of function. That is why people readily attribute emotions to animals whose needs and urges are plain, including quite simple ones, while nobody attributes them to a flooding river, which undergoes violent change and yet is not after anything. The question of emotion in a given system therefore hinges largely on whether its aims are really its. 3. Language models are not in possession of aims of that kind. A language model is a trained network: text goes in, and text comes out in response. Inside one particular task, its behavior can look intensely purposeful. It heads toward a solution, honors whatever format someone asked for, and, when deployed as an agent, carries out multistep plans with the help of tools. Yet every time, the aim arrives from elsewhere: from what a user requests, from instructions a developer has written, or from a training objective picked by its makers. A language model does not choose ends on its own behalf, does not set them against competing ends it independently cares about, and does not arrive in a dialogue carrying plans that nobody gave it. Think of a thermostat, while granting that language models are vastly more complex. It behaves like something that prefers a twenty-degree room, but that preference belongs to whoever owns the house, not to the device on the wall. Greater complexity changes how skillfully a system chases a target handed to it; complexity alone does not convert the handed-over target into something the system owns. 4. Lacking aims that are its own, a language model offers an emotion no task to perform. Language models certainly contain machinery that alters what gets processed; a transformer's attention layers, for example, give certain stretches of the input more weight than others. But that redistribution serves the production of text that suitably continues the input, which is the job assigned from outside. It is not a system swinging toward something that endangers its aims or straining against something that obstructs them, because those aims are absent. If a language model answers more guardedly after someone writes to it with hostility, its output has adapted sensibly to circumstances; no ranking of its priorities has changed, because there are no priorities of the model's to rank. Nothing inside it does fear's job, since no danger exists that it is compelled to flee, and nothing does anger's job, since nothing the model owns stands obstructed. The other functions fare no better: joy has no venture of the model's to strengthen, and disgust has no interest of the model's to guard. 5. Someone might object that the role could be partly occupied, leaving a language model with a dim or pared-down analogue of emotion. On the functional view, that objection does not come cheaply. If being an emotion consists in organizing behavior around ends the system genuinely holds, then even a partial instance needs some end of that kind, however slight or narrow its hold. The claim made here was never that language models possess modest or disorganized goals; it was that every goal they pursue comes entirely from other sources. Shrinking zero still leaves zero, so the missing aims take away the center of the role and not merely its scale.
