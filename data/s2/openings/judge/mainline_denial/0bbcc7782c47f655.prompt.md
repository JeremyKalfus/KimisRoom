A. Next-token prediction and learned emotion concepts: representing an emotion is not having one
   A language model is trained only to predict the next token, and doing that well requires it to build internal representations of emotion concepts, just as it builds representations of chemistry or chess. B. No body: emotions need interoception and physiological change
   In animals an emotion is built out of bodily changes, such as heart rate, hormone levels, breathing and muscle tension, together with the brain sensing those changes from the inside. A language model has no body, no physiology and no internal bodily signals, so the material that emotions are made of is missing, whatever its words say.

   Emotions and moods are states that last: they start, persist, colour what comes after them and fade over time. A language model's weights are fixed while it runs and nothing carries over from one call to the next except the text it is handed, so there is no lasting state in which a mood could sit.

D. Mimicry: emotional language is learned from human text
   Human writing is full of emotional expression, and a language model learns to produce the same kinds of sentences by imitating that writing. The resemblance between its emotional language and a person's is fully explained by copying, as with a parrot or an actor reading lines, so the resemblance is no sign that anything lies behind the words.

E. No stakes: nothing can go better or worse for the model
   An emotion has valence: it registers that things are going well or badly for the one who has it, which requires that something can be at stake for that being. Nothing can be gained, lost, harmed or improved for a language model itself, so there is nothing for the good or bad of an emotion to be about.

F. Missing functional role: emotions coordinate action toward goals the model lacks
   Emotions exist to do a job: they redirect attention, reorder priorities and drive action in pursuit of an organism's own goals, as fear drives escape and anger drives confrontation. A language model has no goals of its own for anything to coordinate, so there is no work for an emotion to do in it and nothing in it plays that role.

Language models are made out of that material. During training, a language model is exposed to a vast body of text written by people, and from it the model picks up how to generate fresh passages of the same sorts, each one suited to its surroundings in the way a human writer's sentence would be. The training does not set emotional wording apart or handle it differently. Consoling someone is acquired by a language model through the very same mechanism as drafting a cooking method, summarizing a court ruling, or narrating a football match. What gets absorbed is regularity: which sentiments tend to surface in which circumstances, the phrases used to voice them, and the way those phrases adjust to formality, closeness and listener. The upshot is real skill with the vocabulary of feeling. Hand a language model a story about loss and it will respond with consolation. After an extended exchange, it might mention that the conversation was a pleasure. Presented with an ingenious proposal, it might call the proposal thrilling. Such replies are frequently graceful, well judged and even touching, and that is because the people whose words supplied them wrote gracefully, with good judgment, and in ways that touched their readers. 3. Consider, then, a language model writing "I'm so glad I could assist" or "this really worries me." We do not lack an explanation for that line. Lines of that kind turn up in human prose whenever circumstances resemble the present ones, and the model was trained to generate them again. Nothing extra is needed to get from the training to the sentence. That its feeling-words match ours is no puzzle demanding some hidden cause; it is precisely the output that copying yields. A system that reproduces human speech faithfully enough, over a wide enough range of settings, will inevitably come to resemble humans, among them humans overwhelmed by emotion. How feeling sounds is one of the things that gets reproduced. 4. Compare everyday cases where nobody hesitates to accept this sort of account.
