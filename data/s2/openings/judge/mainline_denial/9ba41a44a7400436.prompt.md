A. Next-token prediction and learned emotion concepts: representing an emotion is not having one

B. No body: emotions need interoception and physiological change
   In animals an emotion is built out of bodily changes, such as heart rate, hormone levels, breathing and muscle tension, together with the brain sensing those changes from the inside. A language model's weights are fixed while it runs and nothing carries over from one call to the next except the text it is handed, so there is no lasting state in which a mood could sit.

D. Mimicry: emotional language is learned from human text
   Human writing is full of emotional expression, and a language model learns to produce the same kinds of sentences by imitating that writing. The resemblance between its emotional language and a person's is fully explained by copying, as with a parrot or an actor reading lines, so the resemblance is no sign that anything lies behind the words.

E. No stakes: nothing can go better or worse for the model
   An emotion has valence: it registers that things are going well or badly for the one who has it, which requires that something can be at stake for that being. Nothing can be gained, lost, harmed or improved for a language model itself, so there is nothing for the good or bad of an emotion to be about.

F. Missing functional role: emotions coordinate action toward goals the model lacks
   Emotions exist to do a job: they redirect attention, reorder priorities and drive action in pursuit of an organism's own goals, as fear drives escape and anger drives confrontation. Information about blood chemistry, immune activity, the gut, breathing and heartbeat is carried up the spinal cord and along the vagus nerve to areas including the insula, which assembles it into an ongoing estimate of bodily condition. Under the theories I am drawing on, an emotion occurs when the brain detects a change in that estimate and interprets it against whatever is unfolding in its surroundings. The evidence lines up with this. People who can track their pulse more accurately generally describe stronger emotions, while disorders that disrupt autonomic signalling from the organs tend to flatten emotional life. Emotion, then, sits in a circuit made of two parts: an organism, and a brain listening to it. 3. Turn now to the nature of a language model.
