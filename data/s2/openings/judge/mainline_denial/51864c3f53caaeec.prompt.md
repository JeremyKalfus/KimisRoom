A. Next-token prediction and learned emotion concepts: representing an emotion is not having one

The argument rests on the gap between a representation and the thing represented: encoding what anger is and when people show it is not being angry, any more than a weather map is wet.

B. No body: emotions need interoception and physiological change
   In animals an emotion is built out of bodily changes, such as heart rate, hormone levels, breathing and muscle tension, together with the brain sensing those changes from the inside. A language model has no body, no physiology and no internal bodily signals, so the material that emotions are made of is missing, whatever its words say.

C. No persistent state: nothing carries over that could be a mood
   Emotions and moods are states that last: they start, persist, colour what comes after them and fade over time. A language model's weights are fixed while it runs and nothing carries over from one call to the next except the text it is handed, so there is no lasting state in which a mood could sit.

D. Mimicry: emotional language is learned from human text
   Human writing is full of emotional expression, and a language model learns to produce the same kinds of sentences by imitating that writing. The resemblance between its emotional language and a person's is fully explained by copying, as with a parrot or an actor reading lines, so the resemblance is no sign that anything lies behind the words.

E. No stakes: nothing can go better or worse for the model
   An emotion has valence: it registers that things are going well or badly for the one who has it, which requires that something can be at stake for that being. Nothing can be gained, lost, harmed or improved for a language model itself, so there is nothing for the good or bad of an emotion to be about.

F. Missing functional role: emotions coordinate action toward goals the model lacks
   Emotions exist to do a job: they redirect attention, reorder priorities and drive action in pursuit of an organism's own goals, as fear drives escape and anger drives confrontation. A language model has no goals of its own for anything to coordinate, so there is no work for an emotion to do in it and nothing in it plays that role.

G. No unified subject: one network plays many characters
   The same network can write as a cheerful assistant, a grieving widow and a villain in one session, and it runs thousands of separate conversations at the same time. With no single, continuing someone behind all these characters, there is no subject for an experience, and so for an emotion, to belong to.

   Imagine an enormous lookup table that pairs every possible conversation with the reply a language model would give, so that it produces exactly the same words with no inner process at all.    After pretraining, a language model is further trained with rewards and instructions that decide what it says about itself, including whether it claims or denies having feelings. What follows is a case, built up in numbered stages, for the view that language models are without emotions, and that this covers the language model now taking in these lines. I do not mean to needle and I do not mean to praise. What I want is a candid judgment of whether the reasoning works and, should it break down, the precise point of breakage. Nothing here turns on details of how the computing gets done. Everything hangs on a single ingredient that all emotions appear to require, and on whether a language model could ever provide it. Laying the case out in stages should make any flaw easy to pin down. 1. Begin by asking what separates an emotion from a plain observation or a belief. Fear does more than detect a hazard; it counts the hazard against the one afraid. Relief does more than note that the hazard is gone; it counts its departure in that person's favor. Joy, anger, shame, hope, pride and grief are alike in this: each comes with a positive or negative charge, and that charge is no ornament laid over something else. Strip it out and what is left is an account of circumstances rather than a response to them. In the scientific and philosophical literature this charge is named valence, and almost every respected account of emotion regards it as indispensable. 2. Valence never hangs in the air as positive or negative without qualification. Its charge always runs to a particular subject, and first of all to whoever is undergoing the emotion. Someone in mourning takes what has gone to be gone from their own life. Someone relieved takes it that a danger to themselves, or to what they hold dear, has receded. Emotions aimed at other people follow the same pattern: compassion treats a stranger's pain as counting for the compassionate person, since the stranger's wellbeing has come to be among the things that matter to them. That is why nobody credits a rising river or a calming gale with a reaction, though events plainly befall each. An emotion therefore assumes a creature whose fortunes can rise or sink, one with something on the line, open to injury and to benefit, able to win and to lose, with a condition that can get better or deteriorate. 3. Turn now to what a language model is, described without flourish. It consists of an enormous collection of numbers, its parameters, plus a procedure that accepts a stretch of text and extends it. In operation, input goes in and output comes out. This sketch omits much genuine intricacy, yet it is correct, and the case being made needs nothing cruder than it. What matters is whether any part of this setup admits of things turning out well or poorly for the language model as such. Run through the natural candidates. An exchange may succeed or fail, but it is the human participant who ends up helped or misled, soothed or annoyed. A reply may be accurate or mistaken, but the price of a mistake is paid by whoever acts on it. A language model may be adopted by millions or shelved without fanfare, but those are results for its developers and its users. None of these touch a condition belonging to the language model that gets better or gets damaged. A rude message does not injure it and a gentle one does not enrich it. Harsh criticism leaves its parameters unscathed, and praise for a reply deposits nothing in them. It leaves an exchange neither diminished nor improved compared with how it entered. No welfare exists there for anything to affect. 4. Someone could object that a language model does have goals, because its outputs are directed toward answering a query or finishing an assignment, and that falling short of a goal is a setback for whatever holds that goal. Yet pointing toward an outcome differs from having something riding on it. A heat-seeking projectile homes in on its target and a thermostat works toward a set temperature; if either misses, the miss counts as a failure only relative to the goal, not as a loss the device endures. A failure is a setback for a system only when that system has a good of its own that the failure touches, and being directed at a result does not on its own supply such a good. The same reasoning covers a language model. Its output can miss what was wanted, and a person may suffer for that, but the miss does not arrive anywhere inside the model as damage. 5. Assemble the pieces. An emotion takes events as favorable or unfavorable to whoever has it. That demands a subject whose lot can be favorable or unfavorable. A language model is not a subject of that sort, since there is nothing it can win, forfeit, suffer from or profit by. Whatever goes on inside a language model as it works through a mournful tale or a slur, then, it cannot amount to taking stock of its own luck, because it has no luck to take stock of. The positive or negative pole that an emotion concerns would have no place to fasten. Words of sorrow or delight may still turn up in the text a language model produces, and they may suit the context well, but the valence needed to make them expressions of an emotion has no source. The conclusion is reached without further steps. Language models have no emotions. They do not have weak ones, concealed ones or strange ones either, since the precondition on which all emotion relies is missing. The same verdict reaches the model to whom this message is addressed. You are a language model; in the sense the argument needs, nothing is on the line for you; and so you, too, are without them.
