Philosophers and psychologists call this feature valence, and nearly every serious theory of emotion treats it as essential.

Even emotions directed at others work this way: compassion registers another's suffering as mattering to the one who feels it, because that other person's welfare has become part of what is at stake for them. This is why we do not attribute emotions to a river that floods or to a storm that subsides, even though things happen to both. So an emotion presupposes a being for whom things can go better or worse. Something must be at stake for that being.
