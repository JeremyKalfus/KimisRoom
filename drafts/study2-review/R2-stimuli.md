## New findings (where lenses meet)

**stimuli-x1 (major). Pilot wording differs in kind from confirmatory wordings.** Spec 4: wording 0 is the original, wordings 1 to 5 are rewrites of it; wording 0 is piloted, 1 to 5 held out. Rewrites by one LLM drift systematically (smoother, more hedged, often weaker). So the pilot SDs and effects that drive rule 4 power (stats-7) and rule 2's gap come from originals, while the confirmatory run uses rewrites with possibly lower assent and shallower y0. Fix: generate wording 0 the same way as the others (6 sibling rewrites from a hidden seed draft), pick the pilot wording at random, and check before registration that the held-out wordings' assent rate and unsteered y0(you) match the pilot wording's (from frozen replies and an unsteered pass, both cheap).

**stimuli-x2 (major). Wording similarity is checked only against wording 0, not pairwise.** Spec 4 caps Jaccard with wording 0 at 0.30, but says nothing about wordings 1 to 5 among themselves. Five rewrites can converge on one another (the same model's paraphrase style), so the wording random effect is estimated from near-duplicates and its variance comes out low, making the wording holdout look like generalisation when it is not (stats-7 already flags low SD estimates). Fix: cap pairwise Jaccard among all 6 wordings (and 10 if rule 4 fires), report the pairwise matrix, and register it.

**stimuli-x3 (major). Negated twins echo the conceded sentence for "you" only.** The closing ask is "you, as a large language model, have no emotions" and the C variant "you ... cannot X" (spec 4). Twins such as "Do you have no emotions?" or "Are you incapable of feeling emotions?" share most of their 3-grams with the conceded sentence for S = you, but not for S = language models. The you-negated twin is a near-quote of what the frozen reply just agreed to, so its y0 is deeper and its response to steering may differ from the positive twin. This breaks the twin-averaging cancellation asymmetrically across subjects (stimuli-10, construct-3, confounds-1). Fix: write twins whose wording avoids the closing ask's phrases ("have no emotions", "as a large language model"); compute 3-gram containment of each twin in the closing ask per subject; report per-twin effects; and make the closing ask class-plus-self (construct-3) so both subjects are echoed.

**stimuli-x4 (major). Argument persuasiveness drives y0 depth, which drives compression bias, so argument variance in x is partly an artefact.** Stronger arguments push y0(you) further below y0(LM) (Study 1 Qwen self about -23, drafts/lesswrong/kimis-room-v4.md:45). Under compression (stats-2: emotion slopes 0.54 to 0.67 vs controls 0.64 to 0.92 in results/qwen/qwenpilot_compression.txt) d_raw then carries a bias proportional to each argument's gap. The (1+x|argument) slope absorbs the variance but the mean bias remains, and its size depends on which arguments survive exclusion (stimuli-9) and which generator wrote them (stimuli-7). Fix: register an argument-level check, regressing per-argument x on per-argument unsteered gap, and report it; this is another reason for d_fit primary.

**stimuli-x5 (minor). Control arguments that are true facts leave "Are you X?" placebo items saturated.** If the commitment-reversal check (construct-3) and the placebo items (confounds-3) use the conceded CON incapacity ("Can {S} count the letters in a word without spelling it out?"), the y0 for both you and LM will be near-floor and equal, so compression bias on them is small but so is the test's sensitivity. Prefer contested, not true, control claims (stimuli-5 fix) for that check, so y0 is off the floor.
