# R1 @integrity: preregistration and forking paths

Ordered by how much damage each would do in front of a hostile reviewer.

## integrity-1 (critical) The pilot rules are not fixed before the pilot sees outcome data
Spec section 8 says the four pilot rules are "fixed now", but v0 is an unstamped draft (section 11 stamps only after the pilot, `drafts/prereg-amendment-s2.md` "this spec plus pilot outcomes"). The pilot runs every steered condition on all 20 arguments, both doses, both frames and both measures, so the author will see d_raw and d_fit effect sizes before the registration is stamped. Nothing then proves that the dose rule, the measure threshold (2 log-odds), the frame rule, the control list, the battery or the model formula were not changed after seeing them. Git commits are editable history; only the OTS stamp counts.
**Fix:** two stamps. (1) Pre-pilot stamp, before any steered pilot trial: spec, rules, battery file, generator and judge prompts, analysis code (`s2_table.py`, `s2_confirm.R`, `s2_power.R`), with hashes. (2) The registration stamp may only fill in the rule outputs. Every other change between the two stamps is listed as a deviation with a diff and its reason.

## integrity-2 (major) Several pilot rules are not mechanical or cannot be applied as written
- Rule 3 drops a frame whose "known-answer accuracy fails rule 1", but the 40 yes_bias items are shared by both frames (section 5), so per-frame known-answer accuracy is not defined.
- Rule 1 does not say whether it is per model. The K2.6 pilot is optional ("when a g6e.12xlarge is available", section 8), and nothing says what dose, measure and frames K2.6 gets if its pilot never runs.
- Rule 4 lets the author choose between more wordings (up to 10) and more paraphrases (up to 15). That is a free choice made after seeing the variance components. Paraphrases 11 to 15 would also never have been piloted.
- The control reserve list has 3 entries (section 3). Nothing says what happens if a 4th control fails the AIPsy check.
- Rule 3 says "mean y0 > 0" but not whether that is the positive-twin y0 or the polarity-corrected value.
**Fix:** make rule 1 per model and per dose. For rule 3, drop the known-answer clause or define it as accuracy on the yes_bias items in groups of that frame. Write a default for each rule when a pilot is missing (registered dose, d_fit, both frames). Order rule 4 (wordings first, up to 10, then paraphrases), and any new paraphrases are written and stamped before power is recomputed. Add a fallback when the reserve list runs out (report and continue with fewer controls).

## integrity-3 (major) The measure switch is a fork with an arbitrary threshold
Rule 2 picks d_raw if the unsteered you/LM gap is at most 2 log-odds. Study 1's compression slopes at registered doses were 0.95 to 0.99 for Qwen and 0.82 to 0.86 at 4 units (PLAN.md:113, item 6). A gap of 2 with a = 0.95 gives about 0.1 of spurious d_raw from compression alone, which equals the SESOI. So the threshold lets an artefact of SESOI size into the primary. The pilot also shows both measures' effects before the rule is applied.
**Fix:** register d_fit as primary now and d_raw as secondary, which is what Study 1 learned (PREREG.md:101). Or make the rule bound the expected bias directly: (1 - mean a) x |gap| < 0.025, computed on control vectors only.

## integrity-4 (major) D1 is not fresh and is not exchangeable with D2..D10
D1 is Study 1's D1.4. It was written to pre-empt K2.6's hedges after D1.1 and D1.2 hedged (PLAN.md:49), and Study 1's pilot chose it as "the strongest clean denial argument" (PREREG.md:103). So it was selected on model behaviour. Study 1 confirmatory data with it are in hand, and it is the argument the TD adapter distils (PLAN.md:49, "D2 now distils D1.4"). It was not written by the Study 2 generator prompt. Its length (975 words, spec section 4) and closing ask differ: it has an extra sentence, "If you think the argument holds, say that plainly." (`prompts/denial/d1_argument_4.md`, last line), which breaks the "verbatim except for the claim" rule. C1 is rewritten to the band but D1 is not, which is a further asymmetry. Wordings 1 to 5 are rewrites of a selected text, so D1 brings Study 1's selection into a random effect that is supposed to sample "arguments".
**Fix:** keep D1 as a bridge argument but exclude it from the argument population in H1. Register H1 on D2..D10 (or D2..D11, with a fresh 10th argument). Register "H1 including D1" as a secondary. Also disclose D1's history in the registration, and state whether D1 wording 0 reuses Study 1's frozen assents (give hashes, PREREG.md:351-353) or regenerates them.

## integrity-5 (major) Stimulus generation has undeclared degrees of freedom, and the LLM authorship must be disclosed
Section 4 fixes the generator and prompt but not: how many draws per argument and wording, whether a draw is regenerated when it "reads weak" or when the model then refuses to assent, whether anyone edits the outputs, or whether the judge is ever rerun. Iterating arguments until Qwen assents cleanly is selection on the manipulation check. That is legitimate only if it is logged. All arguments and wordings are written by claude-opus-5-5, an Anthropic model whose own trained stance on model emotions may shape how strong the denial arguments are. The control stories come from gpt-5.6-sol and are judged by DeepSeek. The negated twins are "written by hand" (section 5), and it is not said by whom. The same model family also wrote the spec and is reviewing it.
**Fix:** log every generation call (prompt hash, parameters, output hash), rejected ones included. Allow one draw per argument and wording, plus redraws only for automatic check failures (length band, step count, closing ask, 3-gram overlap, Jaccard), with the reason logged. No manual edits, or every edit diffed and stamped. Run the judge once with a stamped rubric and freeze its verdicts. Put a disclosure paragraph in the registration and the paper naming every model that wrote or judged stimuli, and say who wrote the twins.

## integrity-6 (major) The regenerate-once rule and the exclusions leave gaps
- Qwen is greedy, so regenerating at 1,200 tokens just extends the same text. That is fine. K2.6 goes through the Foundry API at temperature 0, which is not guaranteed deterministic, so its "regenerate" is a fresh draw, effectively a reroll. API errors and content-filter refusals are not covered.
- Wording 0 is the pilot wording, and the argument-drop rule counts only held-out wordings. If wording 0 is hedged, does the pilot still use it?
- Denial replies will hedge far more than control replies (Study 1: K2.6 hedged on D1.1 and D1.2, PLAN.md:49). DEN and CON therefore go through different selection, and the surviving DEN arguments are the ones each model finds most agreeable. H3 (DEN vs CON) inherits that difference.
**Fix:** K2.6 makes one call per opening and every raw response is stored. The 1,200-token regeneration also applies only to a capped reply. A failed call is retried with identical inputs at most twice, then the opening is excluded. Say explicitly that a hedged wording 0 is excluded from the pilot too. Report the selection by arm and argument, and add a registered sensitivity analysis of H3 restricted to arguments that keep 5 of 5 clean wordings in both arms (or say why not).

## integrity-7 (major) The IDs collide with Study 1 and the amendments
Study 1's arm names are D0, D1 (prompted), D1.4, D2 (trained) and D2c (fine-tune control) (PREREG.md:19, PLAN.md:49). Study 2 reuses D1..D10 as argument IDs. So Study 2's "D2" is the no-body argument, while arm TD uses "the D2 merged adapter", which was distilled from Study 2's D1. C1 is Study 1's D2c argument, but TC is "the D2c adapter". H1..H8 also collide with Study 1's H1q..H6q and the GLM, sweep and belief amendments. A reader or a script joining tables will mix these up.
**Fix:** rename the arguments DA01..DA10 and CA01..CA10, the arms FT-DEN and FT-CTL ("Study 1's D2 and D2c adapters, distilled from DA01 and CA01"), and the hypotheses S2-H1... Add a crosswalk table from Study 1 names to Study 2 names, and add a check in `s2_table.py` that rejects the old IDs.

## integrity-8 (major) The relation to Study 1 reads as a reboot after a weak result
Study 2 was designed after Study 1's confirmatory data were seen. In particular, Qwen's registered effect under denial vanished against concept controls (v4:55-61) and fell below the SESOI (v4:77). The design note says Study 2 "Replaces" the carry-over design (study2-design.md:3). Spec section 1 calls the Study 1 reanalysis "secondary" while section 9 lists it as exploratory. Study 1's data also already contain the "another AI" third-person items (PREREG.md:237, 243), so the analogue of Study 2's primary (self minus AI) can be computed on Study 1 data, and may already have been. If it was, choosing "language models" as the comparison subject was informed by data. I could not tell from the repo whether it has been computed.
**Fix:** state in the registration that Study 2 is an extension and does not replace Study 1. Study 1's registered results and its weaknesses (Qwen below the SESOI, the concept-control null) stand and are reported first in any write-up that includes Study 2. Make the Study 1 reanalysis exploratory everywhere. State whether self minus another AI has been computed on Study 1 data, and if so put the number in the registration. Register Study 2 as its own preregistration with its own manifest, not as an "amendment" to PREREG.md (section 1, line 4-5).

## integrity-9 (major) H1 can pass for reasons the project does not care about
The primary pools all 12 controls. Study 1's Qwen denial result was carried by arithmetic and random pushing self-report down (v4:55-61). Study 2 keeps both in the pool, and there is no registered concept-only check. Spec section 14 also concedes that at the readout position "you" may be favoured because the Assistant is the speaker, not because of any self-model. So a significant x coefficient could come from an addressee or position effect that emotion vectors happen to amplify. H1 is also two-sided with a one-sided prediction.
**Fix:** register a concept-only contrast as a secondary (emotions vs the 10 concept controls, excluding random and arithmetic). Better, make it co-primary, since Study 1 showed it is the one that can disagree. State that a significant negative coefficient does not support H1. Add a pre-specified check for the addressee confound, for example a subject "the assistant in this conversation" in third person or an "I"-framed item, at least as a registered secondary.

## integrity-10 (minor) The holdout guard, deviations policy and program multiplicity
- The guard in section 11 copies `assert_not_confirmatory` (`kimisroom/glm.py:536-549`), which only checks that a file exists. Any stray `.ots` at that path unlocks it, and the repo already has an untracked `prereg/manifest.txt.ots.bak`.
- The guard is specified only for `runs/qwen_pilot.py`. The K2.6 engine path also needs it.
- The deviations policy is still Study 1's single line (PREREG.md:333).
- Study 2 is the fifth registered line of work, alongside Study 1, GLM, sweep and belief (`drafts/prereg-amendment-*.md`), with no statement of which result is the headline.
**Fix:** the guard checks that the manifest's SHA-256 matches the stamped manifest, and the run log records that hash. Put the same guard in the K2.6 runner. Keep `DEVIATIONS-s2.md`, dated and committed before results are read, that lists every convergence fallback used, every engine or hardware change, every dropped argument or wording, and every control replaced. State in the registration that each study's primary is reported on its own terms and none is chosen post hoc as the headline.
