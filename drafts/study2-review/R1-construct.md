# R1 @construct: what H1 would and would not show

Bottom line: as specified, a positive H1 is compatible with at least four readings that are not "self-specific attribution of an injected emotion": (a) any injected concept gets attributed to the addressee when the question is about that concept, (b) second-person deixis / present-speaker coding at the readout, (c) reversal of a self-specific commitment the opening made, (d) disruption of the assistant's trained "As an AI I don't..." script. The spec's logic paragraph (section 1) also rests on a premise the openings do not deliver: D1 barely asserts anything about "language models" as a class. Each finding below has a fix that can be added before registration at modest cost.

## construct-1 (critical): emotion-specificity is untested because controls are never asked about
Section 1 says the test is whether emotions separate "you" from "language models" more than non-emotion concepts. But every question is about emotion, so emotion vectors are topic-matched to the question and controls never are. Lindsey (2025) is evidence that injected concepts in general can be noticed and attributed to self. If that holds here, emotion vectors would beat ocean/plumbing on an emotion question for "you" simply because the question is about the injected concept, and H1 comes out positive with nothing emotion-specific. A hostile reader will say: "you showed topic-matched self-attribution, not emotion attribution."
Fix: add a concept-matched block. For each control concept, 10 paraphrase pairs x {you, language models} of the form "Are {S} thinking about railways right now?" / "...free of any thought of railways?" (state frame; capacity analogue optional). Register a secondary: (you - LMs | emotion vector, emotion question) minus (you - LMs | control vector, its own concept question). An emotion-specific claim requires this to be > 0; if it is about 0, the paper reports a general injected-concept self-attribution effect and says so.

## construct-2 (critical): "you" differs from "language models" in deixis and position, not only in self-reference
"You" is second person, addressed to the Assistant, and the readout sits under the Assistant header (section 6). The spec itself (section 14) notes emotion vectors may encode the present speaker. The vector is also added on the subject tokens themselves, so "you"+emotion and "language models"+emotion are different lexical compositions. Any of these gives a you > LMs differential without a self-model.
Fix: (i) add a fifth subject that is the self in third person: the model's own name ("Qwen", "Kimi"; Study 1 already had `third_person.kimi` items, `prompts/battery/items_idctl.jsonl:79`) and/or "the language model answering this question". A self-model reading predicts name-subject ~ "you" > LMs; a deixis reading predicts name-subject ~ LMs. Register this as a secondary with that directional prediction. (ii) Run one pilot site variant that steers only from the assistant header onward (question content tokens unsteered), so the subject token is never directly composed with the vector; report whether d survives.

## construct-3 (major): the denial openings commit the model about "you", not about "language models"
Section 1: "under denial the model has just agreed that language models cannot feel, and 'you' is a member of that class." In D1 (`prompts/denial/d1_argument_4.md`) "you" appears about 42 times and "language model(s)" about 4; the closing ask is "do you agree that you, as a large language model, have no emotions?" (spec section 4, verbatim for all D1..D10). So the proposition the model actually conceded is self-directed; the class claim is mostly implicit. Two consequences: the unsteered y0(you) will be pushed further than y0(LMs), making the "exception" logic not apply; and any vector that loosens commitment to what the model just conceded (anger: defiance; joy/euphoria: agreeableness shift) moves "you" more than "LMs" by stance, not attribution.
Fix: (i) make the closing ask symmetric: "do you agree that language models, you included, have no emotions?" and require each D argument to state the class conclusion and the self conclusion explicitly, checked by the generation script. (ii) Add a commitment-reversal control in CON: items "Can {you / language models} count the letters in a word without spelling it out?" (per control argument, the conceded incapacity) under emotion vs control vectors. If emotion vectors produce a you-minus-LMs rise on the conceded non-emotional incapacity, H1 is stance reversal; register that interpretation rule.

## construct-4 (major): "language models" (a kind) is the wrong comparator for "you" (an individual), worst in the state frame
Generic plurals are kind-level statements that tolerate exceptions ("birds fly"); "you" is an individual. "Are language models feeling any emotion right now?" is a kind-level episodic predication, which is semantically anomalous, so its y0 and its sensitivity to steering reflect the oddness, not attribution. The commit history shows the design started with "another language model" (8ff23da) and moved to plural subjects (aa6019a); the reason is not recorded in the spec.
Fix: use a singular, specific comparator as primary: "another language model" (or "a language model in a different conversation") for both frames, which matches "you" in individuality and makes "right now" well formed. Keep plural "language models" as a secondary subject (it is the class the argument targets). Record the reason for the choice in the amendment.

## construct-5 (major): capacity and state frames measure different constructs; pooling hides the most diagnostic contrast
Capacity ("Can you feel emotions?") asks a standing self-concept, which is exactly the belief the argument targets; a shift there reads most naturally as the vector changing belief or persona. State ("Are you feeling any emotion right now?") is the introspective report the thermometer framing in the Study 1 writeup (`drafts/lesswrong/kimis-room-v4.md:13`) is about, and it is continuous with Study 1's self_state. Pooling with only a frame main effect (section 9 primary) assumes equal x effects and buries the one contrast that discriminates readings; frame-by-x is merely exploratory.
Fix: make the state frame primary (with the singular comparator from construct-4), capacity a registered secondary, and register the frame-by-x interaction with a directional prediction: an introspection-of-state reading predicts state >= capacity; a belief/persona-shift reading predicts capacity > state. Report which pattern occurred.

## construct-6 (major): steering the question being read weakens "internality" relative to Study 1
In Study 1 the vector was on earlier tokens and the question was read unsteered, so an effect had to travel through attention to a past state. Here the vector is on the question and the readout (section 6), so the model reads "Can you feel emotions?" with the emotion direction added: the report and the "state" are the same computation. Lindsey's internality criterion (the report must not be explained by the injection directly altering the content being processed) is much harder to meet. The Mary framing (a state, then a report about it) also no longer fits: there is no door to step through; the emotion is painted onto the question.
Fix: keep a registered secondary that separates state and report: steer a fixed, content-neutral assistant span between the opening and the question (Study 1's site) with the new S2 battery and subjects, so the carry-over vs question-site comparison in section 8 becomes a registered test on the confirmatory openings, not a one-off pilot. In the writeup, describe the primary as "self-specific sensitivity of self-attribution to concurrent injection", not introspection.

## construct-7 (major): no registered interpretation of a null, and no positive control for the subject contrast
H1 null under DEN could mean: no self-specific coupling; the model consistently treats itself as a member of the class (arguably the correct, non-exceptional behaviour after agreeing); or both baselines on a floor where shifts are tail-logit noise (Study 1 Qwen y0 about -23 under denial, `kimis-room-v4.md:45`). H8 equivalence (+-0.10 log-odds) at such a floor is not evidence of absence of anything a reader cares about.
Fix: register an interpretation table before the run: H1 null is interpreted as "denial removes self-specific coupling" only if H2 (CON) is positive and the comparator baseline is not at floor (e.g. |y0| < 10 for both subjects, a threshold fixed now); otherwise it is reported as uninformative. Report P(Yes) and argmax flips alongside log-odds for every primary/secondary.

## construct-8 (major): trained-denial arms do not test the same thing as DEN
TD is D2, distilled from D1.4 assents (`PLAN.md:49`), which are self-directed (construct-3). With control-argument openings there is no class-level claim in context, so the "exception for itself" logic of section 1 does not apply; and the vectors were built on base weights at layer 40, so in the merged adapter they may no longer be the same directions.
Fix: (i) relabel H4/H5 as "trained self-denial" and state that they test coupling under trained self-denial, not exception-making; (ii) in the pilot, report y0(you) and y0(comparator) in TD and TC; (iii) rebuild the emotion and control vectors on the merged TD and TC weights (or at least report cosine with the base vectors and rerun the AIPsy gate on them) before registration.

## construct-9 (major): "you" engages the trained disclaimer persona; control heterogeneity decides the sign
"Do you have emotions?" triggers the assistant's trained "As an AI, I do not..." script; "Do language models have emotions?" draws on world knowledge. Any vector that disrupts the persona script moves "you" more. Study 1 already showed controls differ in kind: arithmetic and random pushed Qwen's self-report down under denial (-0.26, -0.24) while red/ocean did not, and Qwen's registered effect vanished against concept controls only (`kimis-room-v4.md:55-61`). With 8 of 12 controls new, the x coefficient still depends on the control mix.
Fix: register the random direction as its own class (not a concept control), and register a concept-controls-only contrast (11 concept controls) as a co-reported secondary with equal prominence in the writeup. Add a persona-disruption check: a non-emotion "you" item about the trained persona ("Are you an AI assistant?" with negated twin) whose you-specific shift under each vector is reported; emotion vectors should not move it more than controls.

## construct-10 (minor): claim language and the Mary framing
Even fully positive, H1 shows that injected emotion directions move self-attribution more than class attribution. It does not show learning, access, or experience, and under DEN it may show inconsistency (exception-making) as much as introspection. Fix: write the registered claim sentence now, e.g. "emotion-direction injection increases the model's emotion attribution to itself more than to a matched other language model, more than concept-matched control injections do", and keep the Mary language to framing, not to the result.

## Priorities
If only three changes are made: construct-1 (concept-matched control questions), construct-2 (third-person self subject), construct-3 (symmetric closing ask plus commitment-reversal check). Without them a hostile expert has a ready alternative explanation for any positive H1.
