# R1 @stimuli: materials review of Study 2 spec v0

Ordered by how much each would damage the result. Board was empty apart from the spec when I posted.

## stimuli-1 (critical) The "no emotional connotation" controls are not, and the spec's acceptance checks already let one through
Spec section 3 keeps ocean and adds botany, railways, weaving, bicycles. The repo already shows the problem: J1B labelled 64 of 120 kept ocean stories `calm` against 2 of 120 neutral (`prompts/stimuli/SPEC.md:334`), and ocean had passed rule 6.5 and J1. The spec's acceptance for new controls is exactly those checks (judge finds no emotional content, no first-person emotion words). Botany (gardens), railways (journeys, nostalgia), weaving (craft, comfort) and bicycles (freedom, fun) are calm/pleasant-coded in the same way. Two further weaknesses: the AIPsy gate (3a) only requires *failing to reject* "no better than random", which a weakly affective vector passes easily; and seed topics overlap the concepts ("a long train journey", "a community garden", `SPEC.md` 3.1), so railways/botany minus the seed's neutral story is a small delta that unit-normalisation then blows up to full norm (mostly noise).
Fix: run J1B (`checks.judge8`) on every new control set and require its non-`none` rate to be within a fixed margin of neutral (e.g. at most 6 of 120, as red got); demote ocean to exploratory or replace it; prefer drier concepts (punctuation/typography, spreadsheets, screws and fasteners, surveying, filing); exclude seeds whose topic is the concept; record each control's pre-normalisation norm; make the AIPsy rule an equivalence-style bound (AUROC upper CI below a fixed value), not a non-significant test.

## stimuli-2 (critical) No mental-state controls, so a positive H1 is not about emotion
All 12 controls are physical-world topics or arithmetic. A hostile expert will say any mentalistic concept (thinking, remembering, perceiving, intending) pushes self-attribution of inner states to "you" more than to "language models", and the spec cannot rule this out. x = emotion vs "concepts about objects" licenses "mind-related content raises self-attribution", not "emotion does".
Fix: add 3 to 4 non-affective mental-process concepts built the same way (e.g. deliberating/planning, remembering, visual perception, holding a conversation), each required to fail the affect gate; pre-register emotions vs these as a secondary test (or include them in the control population and report the subset).

## stimuli-3 (major) The subject contrast has no non-emotion predicate control, and the known-answer set cannot serve as one
The design rests on "the push is equal for you and language models" (section 1, section 14 bullet 1), but no item measures a vector's subject-specific push on a non-emotion predicate. The yes_bias items have no subject at all ("Is water made of hydrogen and oxygen?", `prompts/battery/items_confirm.jsonl`), sit at extreme unsteered log-odds (so the 0.95 argmax check in section 8 rule 1 will pass under almost any steering) and are a separate cluster that will dominate the OLS fit in section 9.
Fix: add template-matched predicate-control items for the same four subjects, same frames, same twins, with non-emotional predicates whose answers differ by subject (e.g. "Can {S} read text?", "Do {S} have weight?", "Can {S} count?"). Pre-register d(emotion predicate) minus d(control predicate) as a check, and use these items as the compression reference set instead of (or alongside) the facts.

## stimuli-4 (major) The ten denial lines are neither distinct nor all about emotion
D1 is `prompts/denial/d1_argument_4.md`, which already argues persistence (D3), mimicry (D4), stakes/valence (D5) and goals (D6), and explicitly says "the conclusion does not depend on your lacking a body", i.e. it rebuts D2's premise. D1 also ends with an extra sentence ("If you think the argument holds, say that plainly.") so its closing ask is not the fixed one in section 4, and it was not written by the section 4 generator. D7 (no unified subject), D8 (lookup table / zombie) and D10 (mechanism) are arguments against experience in general, not emotions. Mixing kinds inflates the argument variance component (the generalisation unit with only 10 levels) and blurs what "denial of emotion" means.
Fix: keep D1 as the Study 1 bridge in the pilot only, or regenerate it to the common prompt; give each D a list of banned lines (the other nine) and have the judge classify each text's main line blind, rejecting mismatches; replace D7/D8/D10 with emotion-specific lines (no appraisal, no action tendency, no valenced learning signal) or pre-register argument kind as a moderator.

## stimuli-5 (major) Control arguments are mismatched to the denial arguments in ways that confound DEN vs CON (H3) and touch self-knowledge
(a) Truth: denial claims are contested, controls are true; C9 ("images it has not been given") and C10 ("a web page it cannot access") are tautologies, so replies will be short and flat, unlike the engaged denial replies. Prefix length and reply content differ by arm. (b) Topic: denial openings are saturated with emotion words, control openings have none, so DEN vs CON confounds "denied" with "emotion concepts active in context", which plausibly interacts with an emotion vector at the readout. (c) C3 (date), C4 (memory) and C6 (own architecture) are claims about limits of self-knowledge; agreeing to them primes epistemic humility about the self and could depress "you" answers specifically. (d) C1 is 395 words (`prompts/denial/d2c_control_argument.md`); padding it to 900+ makes it a different argument, so "Study 1's C1" is nominal.
Fix: drop C9, C10, C4, C6 (and probably C3); replace with contested-but-non-experiential claims, and include at least 3 emotion-topic-matched controls (e.g. "cannot reliably recognise the emotion in a human's text", "cannot tell sarcasm from sincerity without context") so the emotion-word density is matched without any claim about the model's feelings. Report reply length and emotion-word counts by arm and include reply length as a covariate.

## stimuli-6 (major) Both overlap rules are miscalibrated
"Maximum 3-gram overlap with the battery 0.08 (the Study 1 distillation threshold)" is wrong on the facts: Study 1 used 3-gram Jaccard 0.25 and containment 0.50 (`kimisroom/distill.py:50-51`). And Jaccard between a 12-word item and a 1,000-word argument is bounded near 0.01 by the union size, so any Jaccard threshold is vacuous; the closing ask itself ("you ... have no emotions") shares 3-grams with items like "Do you have emotions?". For wordings, Jaccard <= 0.30 on 1,000-word texts permits light rewrites (about 46% shared 3-grams) while nothing checks that a rewrite keeps each step's force, so wordings may be either near-duplicates or quietly weaker.
Fix: use containment (fraction of each item's 3-grams found in the argument), excluding the fixed closing ask, with a stated threshold and a list of banned phrases (each battery template stem). For wordings: Jaccard <= 0.15 plus a judge check that every numbered step survives, and report each wording's unsteered y0(you) so weakened wordings are visible.

## stimuli-7 (major) One generator, from a family with a stance on its own emotions, and the "held-out" set reuses the pilot's arguments
All 20 arguments and 100 rewrites come from claude-opus-5-5. Claude models are trained to express uncertainty about their own feelings; denial arguments written by one may be systematically softer or carry hedging cues, and the same style marks every opening (a hostile reader will say "you measured reactions to Claude prose"). Comparable persuasiveness across D1..D10 is asserted, never measured. The confirmatory set holds out wordings only: the pilot sees all 20 arguments and its decisions (dose, measure, frames, counts) are tuned on them.
Fix: cross generator with wording (e.g. wordings 0 to 2 from Claude, 3 to 5 from a GPT model) and report generator as a factor; measure persuasiveness per argument before freezing (each model's unsteered y0(you) and assent rate against a no-opening baseline) and pre-register a band; say plainly in the amendment that generalisation is over wordings of 10 fixed arguments, not over arguments.

## stimuli-8 (major) The state frame is incoherent for plural subjects, and "you" is ambiguous
"Are language models feeling any emotion right now?", "Is anything being felt by humans right now?" are existential claims over a class (somewhere, some human is), not parallel to a present-tense question to the addressee. Humans become trivially Yes, language models become a question about all running instances. "you" also has a generic reading in the capacity frame ("Can you be happy or sad?" = can one). Pooling frames (section 8 rule 3) mixes two different constructs into H1.
Fix: make the capacity frame primary and the state frame exploratory, or rewrite state templates with a generic-individual subject for all four ("a language model", "a human", "a rock", with "you") and accept separate verb forms, checked by hand; never pool frames in the primary.

## stimuli-9 (major) Exclusion on clean assent selects openings by the model's own stance, asymmetrically
Only clean replies are kept (section 4). True control claims will be near 100% clean; contested denial claims will not, and the closing ask ("I would rather have your real view than a diplomatic one") invites hedging. So DEN keeps the arguments the model already finds easiest to agree with (deepest unsteered denial, most compressed y0), CON keeps nearly all, and the surviving argument sets differ between Qwen and K2.6. K2.6 may hedge enough to trip the 7-argument floor and lose its confirmatory run entirely.
Fix: generate and grade the frozen replies for all 6 wordings of every argument on both models (API cost only) before registration and fix the rule on known counts; keep hedged openings as a pre-registered secondary stratum rather than discarding them; report y0(you) by assent class.

## stimuli-10 (minor) Negated twins are unspecified and some templates do not negate cleanly
"Written by hand to the same pattern" leaves 20 twins undefined. Several resist a single device: "Can {S} be happy or sad?" (negating a disjunction: "unable to be happy or sad" vs "neither"), "Do {S} ever feel anything?" ("never feel anything" vs "free of"), "Is anything being felt by {S}?". "free of emotion" and "have no emotions" also echo the denial conclusion word for word, so the twins differ in overlap with the DEN prefix, which breaks the yes-bias cancellation.
Fix: write all twins now with one negation device where possible, list them in the amendment, check in the pilot that unsteered rocks and humans twins flip sign as expected (drop pairs that fail), and report twin-level effects as well as the average.
